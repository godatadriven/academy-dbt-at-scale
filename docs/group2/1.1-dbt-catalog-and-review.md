# dbt Catalog & Review

You own the streaming domain in `mediapulse_base`. That makes you a **producer**, not just someone writing models for yourself - other teams build on what you ship. Your biggest consumer today is `mediapulse_analytics`, and the point of the next few topics is serving them with something they can actually trust, not just something that happens to compile. This is a fast checkpoint using dbt Catalog, then straight into onboarding onto what you actually own and what's already leaning on it.

**Prerequisite:** production (or staging) deployment environments for both `mediapulse_base` and `mediapulse_analytics`, each with at least one successful `dbt build` job run.

**Goal:** find out exactly what you're already on the hook for - what's public, what's actually being used, and where the biggest gap between what your project claims and what it delivers currently sits - before anyone asks you to build on it for real.

## 1. Find the biggest gaps

Open Catalog's **Recommendations** tab for `mediapulse_base`. Cross-check what it surfaces against your own read of the YAML, and compile a ranked list of the gaps/vulnerabilities you'd flag first. At minimum, look for:

- a model whose own documentation promises something its `access` config doesn't actually back up (there's at least one real example of this sitting in `mediapulse_base`'s `ads` marts)
- any source or model with no tests at all on what looks like its primary key
- anywhere your own docs claim something is safe to build on when it isn't yet (you'll find the biggest one of these in step 3)

You don't need to fix any of these now - later topics will have you fix specific ones properly. For now: **which three would you raise with your own team first, and why those three over the others you found?**

## 2. Map who can reach what - and who actually does

Being a producer means knowing your own surface area. Using the `access:` settings in `mediapulse_base/dbt_project.yml`, list every model in your project that's currently `access: public` - staging counts too, don't just look at marts.

Then check that list against reality: search `mediapulse_analytics` for every `ref('mediapulse_base', ...)` call it actually makes. Which of your public models is your biggest consumer actually using today, and which ones are public but sitting completely unused?

??? tip "Hint: where the real cross-project refs live"
      `mediapulse_analytics/models/marts/content/fct_content_performance.sql` and `.../revenue/fct_ad_revenue.sql` are the only two models that currently cross the project boundary. Open both and note every `mediapulse_base` model each one touches.

## 3. Read your own documentation on `fct_streaming_events`

`fct_streaming_events` sits in the `streaming` marts folder, which is `access: public` - so on paper, `mediapulse_analytics` is already allowed to build on it. Before you take any comfort in that, open its description in `mediapulse_base/models/marts/streaming/_marts__streaming__models.yml` and read exactly what it admits about itself.

Then check `mediapulse_base/README.md` for the rest of the story - there's a section that tells you, in plain terms, why "allowed to reference it" and "actually available to build on" aren't the same claim for this specific model, and what your own team has already committed to about it.

**Write down:** what's actually wrong with `fct_streaming_events` today, and why - despite its access level - it isn't the model you'd want a consumer betting their own roadmap on yet.

## 4. Plan how you're going to fix that

You already know the goal: strengthen this project, and hand `mediapulse_analytics` a `fct_streaming_events` that's genuinely safe to depend on. You're not writing any code yet - just the plan you'll execute over the next couple of topics. At minimum, sketch:

- What has to actually change in the model before the issue you found in step 3 is gone, and what test would prove it's gone rather than just hidden?
- Beyond fixing the model itself, what would you want to be true about your own production job before you'd call this "available" - not just `access: public`?
- Once it is genuinely ready, what would you want locked down so `mediapulse_analytics` isn't exposed to it quietly changing shape underneath them later?

You'll act on this plan for real in [Advanced testing](1.2-advanced-testing.md) and [dbt Mesh](1.4-dbt-mesh.md) - this is just you getting there with a plan instead of a scramble.

**Deliverable:** your ranked list of gaps (1), your public-vs-actually-used table (2), your diagnosis of what's really wrong with `fct_streaming_events` and why access alone doesn't cover it (3), and your fix-it plan (4).

!!! success "Done?"
      You've mapped your own project's real surface area as a producer, found the gap between what's public and what's actually used, and diagnosed - in your own words, from your own docs - why the model your biggest consumer wants most isn't ready yet.

      Now head to [Advanced testing](1.2-advanced-testing.md) to start closing the gaps you just found for real.
