# Modeling Refresh

*Group 1 - Part 1*

A quick refresh of the staging -> intermediate -> marts layering pattern, using real models from `mediapulse_platform` as your walkthrough - not toy examples.

## Exercise

**Goal:** trace one full chain of models end to end and be able to explain, in your own words, what each layer adds.

1. **Pick a chain**
   Open `stg_podcasts__shows.sql`, `stg_podcasts__episodes.sql`, and `dim_shows.sql` in `mediapulse_platform`. Read them in that order.

2. **Name what each layer does**
   For each of the three models, write one sentence describing its job. Staging renames/casts from a source or raw table; what does `dim_shows` add on top of the two staging models it builds on?

   ??? tip "Hint: What to look for in dim_shows"
       Notice it's not just a pass-through join - it aggregates episode counts and dates per show. That aggregation is exactly the kind of logic that belongs in a mart, not in staging.

3. **Check the materializations**
   Look at `mediapulse_platform/dbt_project.yml`. What is `stg_podcasts__shows` materialized as? What about `dim_shows`? Explain in one or two sentences why staging models are typically views while marts are tables.

   ??? tip "Hint: Materializations"
       See the [materializations docs](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/materializations) for the trade-offs between `view`, `table`, `incremental`, and `ephemeral`.

4. **Read a business-logic case study**
   Open `int_episode_listen_completion.sql` and its description in `_int__models.yml`. It caps each listen session's completion rate at 1.0 before averaging. Why does that cap matter - what real-world situation would produce a completion rate over 100% without it?

**Deliverable:** a short written explanation of the three-layer chain you traced, plus your answer to the completion-rate question.

## Extension

Now look at a genuinely more complex intermediate model, and think critically about where logic *should* live - not just where it currently does.

1. **Read the harder intermediate model**
   Open `int_campaign_content_spend_allocation.sql`. Its allocation rule branches on `campaign_type` - brand/awareness campaigns behave differently from performance campaigns, and the denominator itself changes depending on which branch applies.

2. **Argue for the layer**
   Explain why this conditional allocation logic belongs in an intermediate model rather than being pushed directly into a mart, or duplicated across two different marts that both need campaign-level spend.

3. **Find a layering question of your own**
   Open `fct_streaming_events.sql` in the streaming marts folder. It parses `start_date`/`start_time` and `end_date`/`end_time` into timestamps inline, inside the mart itself, rather than in an intermediate or staging model.

   ??? question "Is that the right layer for this logic?"
       Think about it both ways: this timestamp-parsing logic isn't reused anywhere else yet, which is an argument for leaving it where it is - but it's also exactly the kind of transformation staging models are meant to own. What would you need to see elsewhere in the project before deciding to move it?

**Deliverable:** a short write-up arguing your position on the `fct_streaming_events` timestamp-parsing question, with reference to how dbt's own [staging model guidance](https://docs.getdbt.com/best-practices/how-we-structure/2-staging) frames what belongs at each layer.
